Snow Problem: Privacy Policy

Effective October 15, 2026 · Last updated October 15, 2026

Snow Problem (“the game”, “the app”) is a free incremental game about clearing snow, made by Snow Problem Games (“we”, “us”), an independent developer in Ontario, Canada. This policy explains what information the game uses, who else handles it, and the choices and rights you have. We are responsible for the personal information described in this policy, and our privacy contact is listed in section 12. We wrote it to be read, not just filed.

The short version, in plain English
  • What we collect about you: nothing. There are no accounts. We never ask for your name, email or location, and the game has no analytics or tracking of its own. Your progress and settings are saved only on your own device.
  • In the phone app, two companies’ software does talk to its own servers. This is the only personal information involved, and it goes to them, not to us:
    • Google (ads). When the app starts it checks your privacy choices and, if allowed, lines up an optional reward video. Google can receive your IP address, your device’s advertising ID and basic device details at that point, even if you never watch a video. You can say no on the privacy choices screen, and you never have to watch a video to play.
    • RevenueCat (purchases). The app checks with RevenueCat what you have bought, so purchases can be restored. It receives an anonymous app ID, your device type and system version, and purchase details. Apple or Google take the payment; we never see your card.
  • The browser version, if you ever use it, does not use either of these.
  • If you email us, we keep your address and message only to reply to you.
  • We do not sell your personal information, we do not build profiles about you, and we do not share it for any other reason.
  • The game is for people aged 13 and over.

1. What stays on your device

The game keeps your progress, your options (sound, falling snow and similar), a timer for any bonus you earned, and a local note of which purchases you own, in your device’s private storage. This information is not sent to us. If you delete the app, it is removed. A save code you export from Options is a text you control; you decide where to keep it.

2. Information handled by other companies when you use optional features

2.1 Optional reward videos (phone app): Google AdMob

On the phone app, Google AdMob lines up an optional bonus video in advance, so Google’s software contacts Google when the app starts (once your privacy choices allow it), whether or not you end up watching. To do this, Google’s advertising software on your device may collect and use: your IP address (which can indicate an approximate location), device and advertising identifiers (such as the Android advertising ID or the iOS identifier for advertisers), information about how you interact with the ad, and diagnostic information such as crash and performance data. Google uses this to deliver and measure ads, to prevent fraud, and for analytics.

  • Your choices. When the app starts, you are shown a privacy choices screen where the law requires it (for example in the EU, UK and Switzerland, and some US states). On iPhone, the system asks whether the app may track you; if you say no, ads (if you watch any) are not personalised. You can reset or limit your advertising ID in your device settings. Ads are never required to play.
  • Privacy choices later. Where available, Options → SUPPORT contains a button to reopen the privacy choices.
  • Google’s own explanation: Google Privacy Policy and How Google uses information from sites or apps that use its services.

2.2 Purchases: Apple, Google and RevenueCat

There are two optional one-time purchases. Payment is handled entirely by the Apple App Store or Google Play; we never see or store your card or payment details. To unlock what you bought and to let you restore it on a new phone, the app contacts RevenueCat when it starts and when you buy or restore. RevenueCat receives information about your purchases (such as which product you bought and when, and the store receipt or purchase token), basic device and app details (such as device type and operating system), an anonymous app identifier, and your locale and currency. Like most online services, it may also see your IP address when your phone connects to it. RevenueCat does not receive your name or email from us. It handles this information on our behalf and stores it on servers in the United States. See RevenueCat’s privacy policy, Apple’s and Google’s.

2.3 Contacting us

If you email us, your message and email address are handled by Google (Gmail), and we use them only to reply to you and keep a reasonable record of the request. Please do not send us sensitive information, since the game never needs it.

2.4 This website

This page is hosted on GitHub Pages. GitHub may record technical information about visitors, such as IP addresses, as described in the GitHub Privacy Statement. We add no analytics or cookies of our own to this page.

3. What we do not do

  • We do not require an account, a name or an email address to play.
  • We do not sell personal information, and we do not share it for advertising other than the optional videos described above.
  • At the time of writing, the game does not include analytics or crash-reporting tools of its own. If we ever add one, we will update this policy before the change reaches you.
  • The game itself does not collect precise location, contacts, photos, microphone or camera data. (As described above, Google may work out an approximate location from your IP address when an optional video is shown.)

5. Sharing

We share information only with the service providers named above (Google, Apple, RevenueCat and GitHub), who may use it only to provide their services, or where the law requires it or we need to protect our legal rights. If our business is ever sold or transferred, this policy and its protections would continue to apply to any information involved. We do not sell personal information.

6. How long information is kept, and deleting it

  • On your device: until you delete the app or use Options → DATA → Reset save.
  • Emails to us: kept only as long as needed to answer you and keep a reasonable record, then deleted. Ask us to delete yours at any time.
  • Ad and purchase data: held by Google, Apple and RevenueCat under their own policies and retention periods. We have no account system, so there is nothing of yours for us to look up; to limit ad data, reset your advertising ID in your device settings. RevenueCat holds purchase records on our behalf, so for those you can ask us to pass a deletion request on to RevenueCat.

7. Your choices and rights

You can: say no to the privacy choices screen and to tracking; skip every video; reset your advertising ID; restore or ignore purchases; and delete the app.

Depending on where you live, you may also have the right to ask what information is held about you, to have it corrected or deleted, to withdraw consent, to object to or restrict some uses, and to complain to a privacy regulator. This includes rights under Canada’s Personal Information Protection and Electronic Documents Act (PIPEDA) and Quebec’s privacy law, the EU and UK GDPR, and the California Consumer Privacy Act and similar US state laws (we do not sell or share personal information as those laws define it). To use any of these rights, email us at the address below. We may need to confirm who you are first. We will answer within 30 days (if the law lets us take longer, we will tell you why) and we do not normally charge a fee. If we cannot do what you ask, we will explain why.

You can complain to us first, and you always have the right to complain to a regulator. In Canada that is the Office of the Privacy Commissioner of Canada; in Quebec, the Commission d’accès à l’information du Québec. In the EU or UK you can contact your local data protection authority.

8. Children

Snow Problem is for people aged 13 and over and is not directed at children under 13. We do not knowingly collect information from children under 13. If you believe a child has sent us information, tell us and we will delete it. Users between 13 and the age of majority where they live should have a parent or guardian’s permission before making purchases. In Quebec, a parent or guardian’s consent is needed before information is collected about a child under 14, so please ask a parent or guardian before using the optional videos or purchases.

9. Where information goes

We are based in Canada. Google, Apple, RevenueCat and GitHub operate internationally, so information handled by them may be processed in the United States and other countries, under their own safeguards and policies. When information is processed in another country, it can be accessed by courts, law enforcement and national security authorities there under that country’s laws.

10. Security

Those services send information over encrypted connections. We protect our own accounts with the stores and services using strong passwords and two-step sign-in where it is offered. No method of storage or transmission is perfectly secure, and we cannot promise otherwise. If a breach that could harm you affected information we hold, we will tell you and the Office of the Privacy Commissioner of Canada (and any other regulator) when the law requires it, and we will keep a record of the breach.

11. Changes to this policy

When we change this policy we update the date at the top and, for meaningful changes, we also say so in the app’s Help text. If a change is about something new being collected, it is published before the change reaches you.
